01

PanelSafe

Breaker-board auditing from a single photograph.

A YOLO detection pipeline trained on a proprietary field dataset and scaled tenfold by a synthetic generator that crops annotated breakers, masks their backgrounds, and re-pastes them onto rail geometry in new arrangements. Detections are then refined by a spatial heuristic layer and an HMM/Viterbi decoder that applies the physical grammar of a DIN rail.

The interesting part is what went wrong. The model scored 92% and was visibly wrong in the field, confidently calling residual-current devices MCBs. The cause was a six-class rotation between the annotation tool’s classes.txt and the data.yaml training actually read; the model had faithfully learned the permutation. Rescored against corrected labels, that 92% was roughly 6%. The metric had never measured accuracy. It measured agreement with a broken ground truth.

02

PanelSafe Unifilar

Detection output to a schematic an inspector will accept.

Every electrical certificate filed in Spain legally requires a single-line diagram conforming to UNE-EN 60617 and ITC-BT-25. Drawing one by hand in generic CAD costs an installer thirty to forty-five minutes per board, back at the office, disconnected from the panel they just audited.

This engine takes the structured device sequence from the rail decoder and procedurally compiles it into vector SVG in under 500 milliseconds — symbols constructed mathematically rather than stamped from a library, with dynamic multi-pole rendering, a computed load schedule (cross-sections, conduit diameters, calculated power per REBT tables), and vector PDF export for regulatory submission.

Role
Sole engineer — topology inference, symbol engine, vector export
Stack
JavaScript · SVG · vector PDF · shorthand DSL with live re-render
Standard
UNE-EN 60617 · ITC-BT-25 — the symbol set Spanish inspection actually uses

That order of operations is the whole argument for hiring me. I did not learn computer vision and then look for a domain. I had the domain, the access, and a bottleneck expensive enough to justify solving, and I went and got the machine learning. It means I can label my own data correctly, I know which failure modes matter on a real job site, and I can tell the difference between a model that scores well and a model that works.

I train and deploy on my own hardware — Proxmox with GPU passthrough, Kubernetes for inference services — so I own the full path from a photograph on site to a container serving a prediction.
