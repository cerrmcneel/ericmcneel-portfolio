Case study

PanelSafe Unifilar: from detected devices to a diagram an inspector will accept

Procedural UNE-EN 60617 symbol construction, a shorthand notation electricians can actually type, and vector PDF export — all client-side, in under half a second.

Role
Sole engineer — symbol engine, DSL, export
Stack
JavaScript · SVG · vector PDF · no backend
Standards
UNE-EN 60617 · ITC-BT-25 · REBT
Status
Live at panelsafe.cv/unifilar
1.0

A legal requirement, drawn by hand, forty minutes at a time

Every electrical certificate filed in Spain — the Boletín Eléctrico or CIE — and every Technical Design Memorandum submitted to a regional industry authority legally requires an accurate single-line diagram conforming to UNE-EN 60617 and ITC-BT-25.

For a certified installer this is pure bottleneck. After auditing a distribution board on site, you return to the office, open generic CAD software, and spend thirty to forty-five minutes dragging symbols, labelling wire gauges and calculating parameters. The tools are cumbersome, typographically error-prone, and completely disconnected from the physical board you just photographed.

Remark

The filing is not optional and the drawing is not creative work. That combination — mandatory, repetitive, rule-governed — is what makes it worth automating.

2.0

Detections have positions; a schematic needs topology

A detector tells you that there is a 40A two-pole device at these pixel coordinates. A diagram needs to know that it is the main disconnect, that the surge protector sits behind it, that the residual-current device downstream protects five branch circuits, and which of those branches is the kitchen.

The ordered device sequence produced by YOLO detection and the spatial rail layer in PanelSafe is exactly that missing topology. This engine consumes it and procedurally compiles a vector SVG diagram and technical load table in under 500 milliseconds.

Upstream

Ordering devices along the rail is what makes topology recoverable from a photograph at all. Without it there is nothing here to compile.

3.0

Symbols constructed, not stamped

The UNE-EN 60617 symbols are built mathematically in SVG rather than pulled from a bitmap or clip-art library: main switches (IGA), surge protectors (PCS/SPD), differential RCDs (ID), and branch circuit breakers (PIAs), with dynamic multi-pole rendering for 2P and 4P variants.

Constructing them means they scale to any output resolution without artefacts, recolour cleanly, and stay correct when a device changes pole count — which a stamped symbol cannot do without a second asset.

Shorthand DSL — esquema unifilar
// Head of the installation
[IGA:40A:2P] -> [SPD] -> [RCD:40A:30mA:2P]

// Standard branch circuits (REBT ITC-BT-25)
[PIA:10A:1.5mm:T18] -> C1 (Alumbrado)
[PIA:16A:2.5mm:T20] -> C2 (Tomas Generales)
[PIA:25A:6mm:T25]   -> C3 (Cocina y Horno)
[PIA:20A:4mm:T20]   -> C4 (Lavadora / Lavavajillas)
[PIA:16A:2.5mm:T20] -> C5 (Tomas Bano y Cocina)
[PIA:16A:2.5mm:T25] -> C13 (Vehiculo Electrico)

The shorthand exists because detection will never be perfect and an electrician standing at a board should not have to fight a mouse to correct it. Typing a line re-renders the diagram immediately.

Remark

The notation follows how installers already talk about circuits — C1 through C13 are the REBT circuit designations, not an invention of mine. Adoption cost is close to zero.

4.0

The load schedule computes itself

Alongside the diagram the engine derives the technical schedule the filing requires: calculated power per circuit, circuit voltage, minimum copper cross-section in mm², conduit diameter in mm, and nominal circuit lengths, all against the REBT tables.

Compliance metadata is configurable per regional registry — Comunitat Valenciana, Cataluña, Andalucía, Madrid — because the header a filing needs is not the same in every autonomous community.

Remark

This is the part that removes typographical errors rather than just time. A hand-typed cross-section that disagrees with the breaker rating is a rejected filing.

5.0

Why it runs entirely in the browser

Shifting schematic generation to client-side vector rendering removes the CAD licence, the render backend, and the round trip. An installer with a phone in a hallway gets a submission-ready PDF without an account, an upload, or a network dependency — and no photograph of a client’s electrical installation leaves their device.
